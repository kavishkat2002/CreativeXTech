## 1. The Core Philosophy of AEO & GEO

Unlike traditional keyword-density SEO, Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) rely on **information gain, direct factual extraction, unambiguous entity relationships, and source credibility**.

Large language models (LLMs) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines ingest web pages and score candidate passages based on:
1. **Passage Extractability (Answer-First Format):** Can the model quote or summarize a definitive answer in the first 1–2 sentences?
2. **Entity Grounding:** Does the page clearly identify *Who* (CreativeX Technology (Pvt) Ltd), *Where* (Rajagiriya, Colombo, Sri Lanka), *What* (AI Development, Custom Software Engineering, ERP Solutions), and *Evidence* (real delivery metrics, verified case studies, client feedback)?
3. **Structured Authority:** Are definitions supported by JSON-LD (`Organization`, `Service`, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`) and linked schema graphs?
4. **Machine-Readable Summaries:** Does the domain provide structured context via `/llms.txt` and semantic HTML5 tags?

## 3. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) Tactical Playbook

### Tactical Rule 1: The First-Paragraph Direct Definition Principle
Every newly implemented service and article page begins with a bold, declarative statement defining what the service is, whom it serves, and what measurable operational outcome it creates. This ensures that RAG embeddings match user intent directly.

### Tactical Rule 2: Citation Anchor Signals
To earn citations in Google AI Overviews and Perplexity:
- Integrate verified statistics (e.g. 20+ delivered projects, 6+ international markets served, 50+ businesses supported).
- Use structured tables comparing alternatives (e.g. Custom ERP vs Off-the-Shelf ERP; Rule-based bots vs Autonomous AI agents).
- Implement explicit step-by-step methodologies (e.g. 4-step delivery lifecycle: Discovery → Prototype → Production Engineering → Measured Iteration).

### Tactical Rule 3: LLM Context Manifest (`/llms.txt`)
Search engines and automated scrapers utilize `/llms.txt` as a high-density, authoritative context manifest. The endpoint is updated to include explicit Q&A blocks, company credentials, technological competencies, and canonical deep links.
